Prices move in a two-way auction: rising to attract sellers, falling to attract buyers, and continuously rotating between balance and imbalance in search of fair value.

On the illustration below, you can see **Balance (Acceptance):** when the market agrees on value, trading concentrates within a price range. This is where two-sided trade occurs and price spends more time in one area. As price moves above value, sellers become more active; as price trades below value, buyers become more active, rotating price back toward fair value.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHaQQhOXAAE0dA-?format=jpg&name=large)

If price breaks outside of that balance, the market enters **Imbalance (Discovery):** a phase when the market disagrees on value, price must move to find the next area where trade can happen. You typically see **directional movement** and less time spent at each price.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHaQlTjWQAA9NLi?format=jpg&name=large)

## Value vs Price

Value = what something is worth Price = what it’s trading at right now

They are often different.

When value and price don’t match, opportunities appear.

If price is below value → it may be undervalued If price is above value → it may be overvalued

**Stock market example:**

A stock is estimated to be worth 100 (value) But it’s trading at 80 (price)

Why? Bad news, panic, or short-term sentiment

What happens next:

Long-term investors see it as cheap → they buy Short-term traders see weakness → they trade the move

Over time, price will either:

- move back toward value
- or value will adjust to match price

**Outside market example:**

Think about a street market selling T-shirts. The seller believes the shirt is worth $30 (value), but customers aren’t buying at $30.

So what happens?

Price drops to $20 → more buyers show up Price rises to $35 → fewer buyers, more hesitation

This is exactly how markets work.

Price moves to **find participation**:

- Lower prices attract buyers
- Higher prices attract sellers

If both sides agree → price stays (balance) If they don’t agree → price moves (imbalance)

## The Bell Curve in AMT

In Auction Market Theory, price distribution over time often forms a **bell-shaped curve**. This curve represents how the market organizes trading around value.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGYPCx7bgAA8VDt?format=png&name=large)

If you rotate a bell curve horizontally and overlay it on a Market Profile, the price-time distribution looks nearly identical. Same thing with Volume Profile.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHFxPIhaIAAo6BC?format=jpg&name=large)

## Key Principles of AMT

- **Price Discovery:** The primary goal is finding the price where the most volume is transacted and the most time is spent (fair value).
- **Balance vs. Imbalance:** Markets spend time in "balance" (ranging/consolidation) when supply and demand are equal. When information causes imbalance, one side becomes aggressive, leading to trending moves toward a new value area.
- **Value Area:** The price range where roughly 68% (one standard deviation) of the day's volume or time is spent.
- **Acceptance vs. Rejection:** If price moves to a new level and holds, it is accepted. If it moves and reverses quickly, it is rejected, indicating unfair price

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGIwG0RaAAAuijv?format=jpg&name=large)

## The Market Profile and Volume Profile view (common AMT tools)

Auction Market Theory (AMT) is the foundation behind tools like:

- Market Profile (TPO)
- Volume Profile

**Market Profile** organizes a session’s trading by price and time.

**Volume Profile** organizes trading by **volume at each price** (how much business got done at each level).

Together they help you see:

- Where the market accepted price (spent time and did business)
- Where it rejected price (moved quickly)
- Where major shifts happened (transition from balance to imbalance)

Quick example

- If price rallies above a recent range but stalls and returns, AMT often frames that as a failed auction, with price likely rotating back toward prior value.
- If price rallies and then **builds a new range**, AMT often frames that as **acceptance** and a new value area.

## POC, VAH, and VAL explained

**POC (Point of Control)**

- The price level with the **highest volume** or **most time spent** during the session.
- On **Market Profile**: the price with the most TPOs (time price opportunities).
- On **Volume Profile**: the price with the highest traded volume.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGYRZLPaoAIC9MT?format=jpg&name=large)

**VAH (Value Area High)**

- The **upper boundary** of the value area.
- Marks the top of the range where approximately **70% of the session's volume** (or time) occurred.
- Price above VAH suggests the market is **testing for new acceptance** higher.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGYS_-ibIAEXnp2?format=jpg&name=large)

**VAL (Value Area Low)**

- The **lower boundary** of the value area.
- Marks the bottom of the range where approximately **70% of the session's volume** (or time) occurred.
- Price below VAL suggests the market is **testing for new acceptance** lower.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGYTGcJacAAVdtd?format=jpg&name=large)

> Example with Fixed Range Volume Profile on the chart below:

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGI6MMTXIAApSIu?format=png&name=large)

How they work together:

The **Value Area (VA)** is the zone between VAH and VAL - this is where the market **agreed on fair value** during that session.

- **POC** sits inside the value area and shows the **core of acceptance**.
- **VAH and VAL** frame the **outer edges** of that acceptance.
- Price spending time **outside** the value area (above VAH or below VAL) indicates either **rejection** (quick return) or **new discovery** (new value forming).

The grey box on the chart is a value area of several days of market profile merged together.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHKGn00WsAAXe0A?format=jpg&name=large)

> **The idea is to buy below value and sell above value.**

Buyers step in below value Sellers step in above value

Buying above value is less attractive Selling below value is less attractive

## 10 Laws of Market Dynamics

**Law 1:** If price accepts into value, it will attempt to traverse the entire value area.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHFzfY2awAAMUi0?format=jpg&name=large)

Quick example demonstrating **Law 1**: price moved back inside the **9-day composite value area**, reclaimed **VAL**, and quickly rotated higher toward **VAH**.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHVTEP8WQAA3zuY?format=jpg&name=large)

**Law 2:** Price accepting outside of value area often leads to continuation of the break.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHKR7uqWgAAU_7Q?format=jpg&name=large)

Quick example demonstrating **Law 2:** price moved above the **9-day composite value area**, reclaimed **VAH**, starting compressing above it and finally pumped higher.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HH3xah9XMAMCGyw?format=jpg&name=large)

**Law 3:** Price often retests areas where condition has shifted (Imbalanced/Balanced)

**Law 4:** Price often trades back to old value.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHKSuDbXEAA-yG2?format=jpg&name=large)

**Law 5:** Price trades inside balance until proven otherwise

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHKVlgrX0AACXhO?format=jpg&name=large)

**Law 6:** Market trades extremely choppy in the middle of the value - don't trade here.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HHKVy37XYAAfX5i?format=jpg&name=large)

**Law 7:** Exception to **Law 1** is a strong rejection from POC
